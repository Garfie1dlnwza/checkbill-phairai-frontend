# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Groups of Thai friends splitting food and drink bills at restaurants. Primary context: phone passed around the table or one person managing entry alone, then sharing the summary receipt to a group chat. Users are non-technical, often in social settings, and want a fast result with no learning curve. They speak Thai; the app must feel natural in that language.

## Product Purpose

Calculate exactly how much each person owes after a shared meal, accounting for who ordered what, quantities, and optional VAT. Success means: every person at the table knows their amount in under 2 minutes, and the summary image is clear enough to drop directly into LINE or WhatsApp without explanation.

## Brand Personality

Casual and precise. Like a sharp friend who handles the bill without making it feel like accounting. Three words: **clear, social, trustworthy**.

The interface should feel premium enough to screenshot and share, but relaxed enough that pulling it out at the table feels natural, not formal.

## Anti-references

- **Generic dark glass app**: Black background + backdrop-blur cards + white/10 borders is the current aesthetic and exactly what this should move away from. It reads as a crypto wallet or SaaS dashboard, not a social dining tool.
- **Overly playful or childish**: No cartoon icons, no rainbow badge systems, nothing that looks like a food delivery app targeting teenagers.
- **Crypto / fintech hype**: No neon gradients, no glass-on-glass stacking, no hero metrics with big glowing numbers.
- **Splitwise clone**: Splitwise works but feels like a debt tracker. This should feel more like a shared moment, not a ledger.

## Design Principles

1. **The table is the context.** Every design decision should pass the "can I do this comfortably while sitting at a restaurant table?" test. Speed and low friction over features.
2. **Numbers are the product.** Currency amounts are the most important thing on screen. They deserve the most visual weight, the clearest font rendering, and the most accessible contrast.
3. **Export is the payoff.** The summary receipt is what users share. It must look genuinely good — not like a screenshot of a data table.
4. **One screen at a time.** The flow is linear: add items → assign people → view summary → share. Design should reinforce this sequence without trapping users.
5. **Thai-first.** Copy, placeholder text, and affordances should feel native to Thai speakers, not translated from English UX patterns.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG AA minimum (4.5:1 contrast for body text)
- Touch targets minimum 44×44pt — used on phones at the table
- Thai script rendering must be sharp at all font sizes (Noto Sans Thai already loaded)
- Reduced motion support for users who have it enabled
